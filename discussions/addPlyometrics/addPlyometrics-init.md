The best way to track plyometrics in a physical notebook is to use a matrix or table format that captures power output, volume, and quality rather than just traditional weights and reps. [1] (https://www.reddit.com/r/Fitness/comments/1ussnk/do_you_bring_a_notebook_how_do_you_organize_it/), [2] (https://www.gymnoteplus.com/blog/gym-workout-notebook)
Plyometrics rely on explosiveness, ground contact time, and fatigue management, meaning your notebook needs to track how you moved, not just that you moved.

1. Essential Metrics to Record

Unlike standard weightlifting where you track weight and reps, plyometrics require specific performance pillars:
• Date & Readiness: Note the date, body weight (if tracking power-to-weight ratio), and a quick 1–5 score for how fresh or heavy your legs feel. [1] (https://jamesclear.com/workout-journal), [2] (https://www.mayooshin.com/workout-journal), [3] (https://www.gymnoteplus.com/blog/gym-workout-notebook)
• Exercise Name & Variation: Be specific (e.g., Depth Jump to Box, Single-Leg Pogo Jump, Countermovement Jump (CMJ)).
• Volume (Sets × Reps): Keep reps low and quality high (e.g., 4 × 5).
• Contact/Height Data (Optional/High-Value): If you measure it, track max height (in inches/cm) via a Vertec or wall-tap, or note distance for broad jumps. [1] (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-PAHyxJd_tU&t=6)
• Rest Intervals: Plyometric power drops fast without adequate rest. Jot down rest times (e.g., 90s or 2 min) between sets. [1] (https://www.gymnoteplus.com/blog/gym-workout-notebook), [2] (https://jamesclear.com/workout-journal)
• Quality / Pop Rating: Rate the explosiveness or "spring" of the set on a simple scale (e.g., 1 = sluggish/slow, 2 = normal, 3 = high pop/crisp). [1] (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-PAHyxJd_tU&t=6), [2] (https://www.gymnoteplus.com/blog/gym-workout-notebook)

2. Setting Up Your Notebook Page

Use a dot-grid or ruled notebook (like a composition book or pocket-sized Portage or Leuchtturm1917 book). [1] (https://lifehacker.com/health/how-to-set-up-a-training-journal-in-a-paper-notebook), [2] (https://www.gymnoteplus.com/blog/gym-workout-notebook)

Example Table Layout

Draw a compact table across your page for each session:

Exercise	Prescribed (Sets × Reps)	Actual (Sets × Reps)	Height / Dist.	Rest	Quality (1-3)
Depth Jumps	3 × 5	3 × 5	24" box	90s	2
Broad Jumps	4 × 3	4 × 3	7'6"	60s	3
Pogo Jumps	2 × 20s	2 × 20s	—	45s	2


3. Best Practices for Plyo Logging

• Prioritize quality over fatigue: If your "pop" rating drops to a 1 or your ground contact gets slow, draw a line or make a note (stopped early due to fatigue). Never grind through messy plyometrics.
• Use shorthand symbols:
	• * for a personal record (highest jump or best distance).
	• ! for a crisp, explosive set.
	• Fatigue or Heavy if your central nervous system felt fried. [1] (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-PAHyxJd_tU&t=6), [2] (https://www.gymnoteplus.com/blog/gym-workout-notebook)
• Weekly reviewing: Dedicate one page per week. At the end of the week, look at whether your jump heights or contact speeds are trending up or if you need an extra recovery day. [1] (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QLmuKtwuvNY&t=10)
